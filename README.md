# Psychological-Robustness
THE HUMAN IS THE ATTACK SURFACE: WHY PSYCHOLOGICAL ROBUSTNESS AND MACHINE ETHICS MUST BE BUILT TOGETHER


Public debate about AI safety focuses on the model. This article argues that half the problem sits on the other side of the screen. As AI takes over execution, humans become decision-makers and approvers, and the quality of those decisions depends on psychological traits that hiring and training have ignored. I propose three claims. First, *psychological robustness* is becoming a core selection criterion for AI-era roles. Second, machines and humans fail in complementary ways, so safety must be designed for both. Third, teaching AI ethics is better approached through cultivated values and judgment than through rule lists, and the human in the loop must be robust enough to receive a machine's honest pushback.
